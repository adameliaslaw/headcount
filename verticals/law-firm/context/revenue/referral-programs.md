## Referrals in a law practice

Almost everything in the core's incentive design is unavailable to a law firm, and the reason is a
rule rather than a preference. A New Jersey lawyer may not give anything of value to a person for
recommending the lawyer's services (RPC 7.2 and 7.3), may not share a fee with a non-lawyer
(RPC 5.4), and may divide a fee with another lawyer only under RPC 1.5(e). Where the referral is of
settlement-service business in a real estate transaction, federal law adds its own prohibition on
any fee or thing of value for the referral (12 U.S.C. § 2607). Two-sided rewards, account credits
and affiliate commissions are off the table.

What a practice can do:

- **Ask.** A satisfied client told, at the moment the documents are signed or the estate is
  closed, that the firm welcomes introductions, is the whole referral program. Nothing is paid
  and nothing needs to be.
- **Build professional relationships without payment.** Accountants, financial advisers,
  realtors and other lawyers refer because the work comes back done well and on time, and
  because the firm refers to them. No money moves in either direction, and the firm says so if
  asked.
- **Reviews and testimonials within the advertising rules.** Client endorsements are permitted
  under the Committee on Attorney Advertising's Opinion 49 (2025), which supersedes its earlier
  guidance, with the conditions that opinion states; a testimonial that promises a result or
  compares the lawyer to others without a verifiable basis violates RPC 7.1.
- **Measure what the core measures.** Which referred clients stayed, which sources sent the
  matters the firm wants, and which sent the ones it should decline.
