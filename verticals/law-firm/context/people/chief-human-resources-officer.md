## Non-lawyer staff in a practice

A law firm's organization has a line through it that the core's org design does not draw: what a
licensed attorney must do personally, and what everyone else may do under supervision. New Jersey
RPC 5.3 makes the attorney responsible for the conduct of non-lawyer staff as if it were the
attorney's own, and the unauthorized practice of law is a statutory offense for the staff member
and an ethics matter for the firm.

The design consequences:

- **The delegation line is a document.** Staff and agents gather facts, prepare drafts from the
  attorney's instructions, assemble documents, compute and calendar dates, and handle logistics.
  They do not give legal advice, quote or negotiate a fee, decide who the client is, sign for the
  attorney, sign a trust account check, or release work product. Every hire reads it on day one
  and every agent is configured to it.
- **Confidentiality training is onboarding, not compliance.** A new assistant learns RPC 1.6
  before they learn the phone system: everything about every client is confidential, inside the
  family too, and a question from a beneficiary or a relative is answered with "the attorney will
  call you".
- **Conflicts travel with people.** A paralegal or associate arriving from another firm brings
  that firm's clients into the conflict check; a screen may be needed, and the check runs before
  the offer.
- **Departures are a file-security event.** Access ends the day the employment does; the trust
  account signature card is updated; client files stay. The exit checklist is the one the
  `security` department would write, with the ethics duty on top.
- **Compensation cannot be a share of fees** for a non-lawyer (RPC 5.4). A bonus tied to the
  firm's overall results is permitted; a percentage of a matter's fee is not.

For a solo practice with one assistant, all of this still applies, and the attorney is the whole
supervision structure.
