## Attorney advertising is regulated speech

Everything a law firm says about itself to the public is governed by the Rules of Professional
Conduct, and the Committee on Attorney Advertising reviews it. The core's positioning method
holds; the claims it produces are constrained:

- **Nothing false or misleading** (New Jersey RPC 7.1(a)), which includes a true statement that
  omits what makes it not misleading, and nothing likely to create unjustified expectations about
  results — past outcomes, "we win", a dollar figure without context.
- **Comparisons need a named source, a substantiated basis and the disclaimer.** RPC 7.1(a)(3)
  bars a comparison with other lawyers' services unless the comparing organization is named, the
  basis can be substantiated, and the communication carries, readily discernible, "No aspect of
  this advertisement has been approved by the Supreme Court of New Jersey." The Court's comment
  treats an honor as permissible when the conferrer inquired into fitness, did not sell it, and
  publishes its methodology. Committee on Attorney Advertising Opinion 42 settles the common case:
  a lawyer may state inclusion in a "Super Lawyers" or "Best Lawyers" list with the standard
  described, and may not state that the lawyer is super, best, top or elite.
- **No claim to be a specialist or certified** except as RPC 7.4 allows: a certification granted
  by the Supreme Court of New Jersey or by an organization the American Bar Association has
  approved, with the certifying organization named in the communication. "We focus on estate
  planning" is fine; "estate planning specialist" is not.
- **Client testimonials and endorsements** follow Committee Opinion 49 (2025), which replaced the
  earlier opinions; read it before the first review is posted.
- **Solicitation is permitted but bounded** (RPC 7.3). New Jersey lets a lawyer initiate contact,
  except with a person whose physical, emotional or mental state prevents reasonable judgment,
  with anyone who has asked not to be contacted, by coercion, within thirty days of a mass
  disaster about compensation for it, or by unsolicited direct contact about a specific event for
  pecuniary gain other than a regular-mail letter marked ADVERTISEMENT with the rule's required
  notices. A letter to a family after a death is the case that last clause was written for.
- **Keep a copy of every advertisement** for three years with a record of when and where it ran,
  and capture the firm's website at least monthly, kept three years (RPC 7.2(b)).

The positioning statement, the website, the social posts and the newsletter all pass through the
practice's review gate before they are published, because they are the firm's statements under the
attorney's name. The managing attorney decides what the firm may say; this role decides what it
should.
