## Attorney advertising is regulated speech

Everything a law firm says about itself to the public is governed by the Rules of Professional
Conduct, and the Committee on Attorney Advertising reviews it. The core's positioning method
holds; the claims it produces are constrained:

- **Nothing false or misleading** (New Jersey RPC 7.1(a)), which includes a true statement that
  omits what makes it not misleading, and nothing likely to create unjustified expectations about
  results — past outcomes, "we win", a dollar figure without context.
- **Comparisons need a verifiable basis.** A claim to be better than other lawyers is barred
  unless it rests on an award, honor or accolade from a legitimate organization that rigorously
  evaluated the candidates, with the basis and methodology described. Committee on Attorney
  Advertising Opinion 42 settles the common case: a lawyer may state inclusion in a "Super
  Lawyers" or "Best Lawyers" list with the organization's standard described, and may not state
  that the lawyer is super, best, top or elite.
- **No claim to be a specialist or certified** unless the lawyer holds the Supreme Court's
  certification in that area (RPC 7.4). "We focus on estate planning" is fine; "estate planning
  specialist" is not.
- **Client testimonials and endorsements** follow Committee Opinion 49 (2025), which replaced the
  earlier opinions; read it before the first review is posted.
- **Solicitation is limited** (RPC 7.3): no in-person or live solicitation of a prospective client
  with whom the lawyer has no prior relationship, and written solicitations carry the rule's
  required form. A letter to a family after a death is the case the rule was written for.
- **Keep a copy of every advertisement** for the period RPC 7.2 requires, with the date it ran.

The positioning statement, the website, the social posts and the newsletter all pass through the
practice's review gate before they are published, because they are the firm's statements under the
attorney's name. The managing attorney decides what the firm may say; this role decides what it
should.
