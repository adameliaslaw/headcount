## The trust account is reconciled under court rule, not at the close

A New Jersey practice keeps at least two accounts — an attorney trust account for money entrusted
to the firm in a representative capacity, and an attorney business account for the firm's own —
in a New Jersey institution the Supreme Court has approved, and the trust account carries that
label on its face (R. 1:21-6). What makes this different from the core's reconciliation
discipline is that the rule sets the cadence and the records, the Office of Attorney Ethics audits
compliance at random, and the knowing misuse of trust funds is disbarment without regard to
repayment.

The mechanics the rule requires:

- **A ledger per client**, so that the balance held for each person is known at any time, and a
  receipts and disbursements journal for the account as a whole.
- **A monthly three-way reconciliation** of the client ledgers against the journals and
  checkbook and against the bank statement, with the reconciliation records kept seven years.
  Unlike the core's close, this one is not shortened by moving work out of the window; it is
  done every month, on the statement, and signed.
- **Withdrawals to named payees only, never to cash**, and only a New Jersey attorney signs trust
  checks. Staff may prepare; the attorney signs.
- **No commingling**, except the minimal personal funds needed to cover the bank's service
  charges, recorded on their own ledger.
- **Fiduciary money is separate.** Funds the attorney holds as executor, trustee or guardian go
  into a fiduciary account for that estate or trust, not the trust account.
- **A disputed fee stays in trust.** The firm's share of entrusted funds may be withdrawn when
  due, but if the client disputes the bill after notice, the disputed portion stays until the
  dispute is resolved.
- **Interest is not the firm's.** Client funds earn interest for the client or for the IOLTA
  program, never for the attorney.

The close for the practice therefore has two cutoffs: the firm's own books, on the core's
schedule, and the trust reconciliation, on the rule's. The second is the one an auditor opens
first.
