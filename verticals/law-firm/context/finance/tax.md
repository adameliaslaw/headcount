## The taxes a practice meets at a death

An estate planning and administration practice works in a different tax landscape from a company,
and most of it is settled by who receives rather than what is earned. The figures below are
New Jersey's and the federal government's as published; confirm the current year's before any of
them goes in a letter.

- **New Jersey transfer inheritance tax.** A tax by class of beneficiary: Class A (spouse, civil
  union or domestic partner, parents, grandparents, descendants, stepchildren) exempt; Class C
  (siblings, a child's spouse) exempt on the first $25,000 then 11% to 16%; Class D (everyone
  else) 15% to $700,000 and 16% above; Class E (charities and the State) exempt (N.J.S.A.
  54:34-2, 54:34-4). Due eight months after death with no weekend carry-over; extensions to file
  only, never to pay; a lien for fifteen years. Waivers from the Division, or the self-executing
  Forms L-8 and L-9 for Class A, are needed to move New Jersey real estate and accounts.
- **No New Jersey estate tax** for deaths on or after January 1, 2018.
- **Federal estate tax.** Form 706 is due nine months after death (26 U.S.C. § 6075(a)), with a
  six-month extension on Form 4768, where the gross estate plus adjusted taxable gifts exceeds
  the basic exclusion amount — $15,000,000 for 2026 under Rev. Proc. 2025-32, indexed. A timely
  706 is also the only way to elect portability of a deceased spouse's unused exclusion, which is
  why one may be filed when no tax is due. The annual gift exclusion is $19,000 per donee for 2026.
- **Income tax of the decedent, the estate and the trust.** The decedent's final Form 1040 and
  NJ-1040; a Form 1041 and NJ-1041 for the estate or trust while it has income; and the basis
  rule that drives much of the planning — property acquired from a decedent takes a basis equal
  to its date-of-death value (26 U.S.C. § 1014), while property received by gift carries the
  donor's basis (§ 1015). A lifetime gift of appreciated property to save inheritance tax can cost
  more in capital gains than it saves.
- **Inherited retirement accounts.** A non-spouse beneficiary generally must take the whole
  account within ten years (26 U.S.C. § 401(a)(9)(H)); a spouse, a minor child until majority, and
  a disabled or chronically ill beneficiary have longer. Income tax is owed as the money comes out,
  so the choice of beneficiary is an income-tax decision.
- **Taxes at a sale of real estate.** The realty transfer fee on the seller, the Graduated
  Percent Fee on the seller for consideration over $1,000,000 (1% to 3.5% of the whole price
  since July 10, 2025), and withholding or an exemption claim for a nonresident seller.

The calendar discipline the core describes applies with a sharper edge: these are not filings
that can be made late with penalties; several of them end rights. `counsel:estate-administration`
holds the clocks.
