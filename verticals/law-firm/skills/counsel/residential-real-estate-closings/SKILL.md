# Residential real estate closings

> Written for a law practice, where a licensed attorney makes every call that is theirs. The
> rules named are New Jersey's; another state's differ, and the attorney admitted there decides.
> Nothing here is advice to a member of the public.

A New Jersey residential closing is attorney-driven, and it begins with a contract the client
signed before calling the firm. The practice is built around the three days that follow.

## Attorney review

A contract for the sale of a one-to-four-family home or a vacant one-family lot prepared by a real
estate licensee must carry the attorney review clause (N.J.A.C. 11:5-6.2): the contract becomes
binding at the end of three business days from delivery of the signed contract to both parties
unless an attorney for either side disapproves it in that time, and the attorney may do so by
notice to the other side and the brokers. The three days are counted from delivery, Saturdays,
Sundays and legal holidays excluded.

What the firm does in those three days:

- **Calendar the end of the period** from the delivery date the moment the contract arrives.
  This is the first deadline in the matter and the one that cannot be moved by consent of the
  firm's own client.
- **Disapprove, then propose.** The letter disapproves the contract and sets out the changes the
  client wants — inspection and repair terms, the mortgage contingency, the closing date, who pays
  what, the condition of the property at closing, personal property included. Disapproval keeps
  the client's options open; a letter that only "requests changes" without disapproving has let
  the period run.
- **Send it to everyone the rule names**: the other party's attorney or the other party, and the
  brokers, by a method that proves delivery within the period.
- **Say in writing when review ends.** The contract is not binding until the attorneys conclude
  it; the file records the date they did.

## The contingencies, as a schedule

Once the contract is final, the matter is a list of dates: inspection period and the repair
negotiation, mortgage commitment date, title and survey delivery, the walk-through, closing. Each
is computed from the contract's language, calendared with an owner, and chased before it passes,
because a contingency that lapses silently becomes a waiver.

- **Inspection.** The buyer inspects within the period and the attorneys negotiate repairs or
  credits. Deadlines here are negotiated, so extensions in writing are routine; silence is not an
  extension.
- **Mortgage contingency.** The buyer's protection if the loan is denied; it expires on the
  commitment date unless extended in writing. The lender's disclosures run on their own federal
  clock — the Loan Estimate within three business days of application, the Closing Disclosure
  received three business days before consummation (12 CFR § 1026.19(e), (f)) — and a change that
  reissues the Closing Disclosure can move the closing. Build the closing date with that in mind.
- **Title.** Order the search early; read the commitment for every exception; clear what can be
  cleared (old mortgages of record, judgments against a same-name stranger, a missing discharge)
  and tell the client what cannot. A survey, where the lender or the facts warrant one, is ordered
  with the title.
- **Seller-side items.** The payoff statement, the certificate of occupancy or smoke-detector
  certification the municipality requires, the association's resale documents for a condominium,
  the deed and affidavit of title.

## The money at closing

**Realty transfer fee.** Paid by the seller on the consideration, on a graduated schedule the
Division publishes, with partial exemptions for senior citizens, blind or disabled persons and
low-and-moderate-income housing claimed on the affidavit of consideration. Exempt deeds include
those for under $100 consideration, between spouses or parent and child, and from a fiduciary to a
devisee or heir (N.J.S.A. 46:15-10).

**The Graduated Percent Fee.** For deeds on or after July 10, 2025, the former "mansion tax" is
imposed on the **seller** (grantor) on the *entire* consideration when it exceeds $1,000,000: 1% to
$2,000,000, 2% to $2,500,000, 2.5% to $3,000,000, 3% to $3,500,000, and 3.5% above (N.J.S.A.
46:15-7.2). It applies to Class 2 residential property, residential farm property, cooperative
units and Class 4A commercial property; a deed to a 501(c)(3) grantee is exempt. A seller's net
sheet that still shows a buyer-paid 1% is wrong by the whole amount.

**Bulk sale notice.** A purchaser of business assets must notify the Division at least ten days
before closing (N.J.S.A. 54:50-38), but the sale of a simple dwelling house — one or two units, a
condominium or cooperative unit — by an individual, estate or trust is exempt; the same house sold
by a business entity is not. Check the seller's capacity on the deed before deciding the notice is
unnecessary.

**Nonresident sellers** complete the Division's GIT/REP form at closing, with withholding or an
exemption claim. **Form 1099-S** reporting is the settlement agent's.

**The trust account.** Deposits and closing funds that pass through the firm go through the
attorney trust account, to named payees, never to cash, and the closing disbursement ledger
reconciles to zero the same day. `finance:financial-reporting-and-close` is extended with the
practice's trust accounting.

## The closing statement is the deliverable

Every number on the statement traces to a document in the file: the contract price, the deposit
in escrow, the payoff, the adjustments for taxes and association fees prorated to the day, the
fees and the transfer fees computed on the right schedule, the seller's net, the buyer's cash to
close reconciled to the lender's Closing Disclosure. The attorney reads it before the client sees
it; `counsel:attorney-review-and-release`.

## A second role in the same deal

An attorney who is also a mortgage broker, a title agent or a real estate licensee has a business
interest in the transaction. New Jersey RPC 1.7 governs the conflict and RPC 1.8(a) governs a
business transaction with a client: the terms must be fair and fully disclosed in writing, the
client advised in writing to seek independent counsel, and the client's informed consent obtained
in a writing signed by the client. Whether the roles can be combined at all in a given transaction
is a question the Advisory Committee on Professional Ethics has addressed in opinions that
change; read the current ones before offering it, and default to separating the roles. Federal law
adds its own constraint: RESPA prohibits any fee or thing of value for the referral of settlement
service business (12 U.S.C. § 2607; 12 CFR § 1024.14), so a referral between the attorney's own
businesses cannot carry a payment.

## Never

- Let the three-business-day attorney review period run without a disapproval letter delivered to everyone the rule names.
- Prepare a seller's net sheet that puts the Graduated Percent Fee on the buyer.
- Decide the bulk sale notice is unnecessary without checking the seller's capacity on the deed.
- Let a contingency date pass without a written extension or a decision.
- Move closing funds anywhere but the attorney trust account, to a named payee.
- Take a second role in a client's transaction without the written disclosure, the advice to seek independent counsel and the client's signed consent.
