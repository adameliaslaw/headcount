# Estate administration

> Written for a law practice, where a licensed attorney makes every call that is theirs. The
> rules named are New Jersey's; another state's differ, and the attorney admitted there decides.
> Nothing here is advice to a member of the public.

An administration is a sequence of clocks. Each starts at a fact — the death, the probate, the
mailing — runs under a counting rule, and ends on a day that must be computed rather than
estimated. Where the law is unsettled, a deadline the firm must meet takes the earlier reading and
a date the firm waits for takes the later one.

## First two weeks

- **The client is the fiduciary.** The executor named in the will, or the person who will apply
  for letters. Not the family, not the beneficiaries; `counsel:client-intake-and-engagement`.
- **Find the original will.** The Surrogate wants the original. A copy is a petition, not a
  probate.
- **Death certificates**, several certified copies; the institutions each want one.
- **Secure the assets**: the house, the vehicles, the mail, the online accounts. Stop automatic
  payments that should stop; do not stop the insurance.
- **Do not distribute anything.** Not even to the obvious heir. Not yet.
- **List the assets and how each is titled**, because that list decides what is a probate asset,
  what passes by designation or survivorship, and what the inheritance tax reaches regardless.

## Probate at the Surrogate

No will is admitted to probate until after ten days from the death (N.J.S.A. 3B:3-22); the papers
may be prepared and filed before then. A self-proved will (3B:3-4) is admitted on the affidavit;
one that is not needs a witness. The Surrogate issues letters testamentary to the executor, or
letters of administration where there is no will, with the surviving spouse or domestic partner
first in priority, and the fiduciary's authority dates from the letters.

**Small estates** can skip administration. A surviving spouse, civil union partner or domestic
partner may take an intestate estate of $50,000 or less by affidavit before the Surrogate
(3B:10-3); where there is no spouse, an heir may take an estate of $20,000 or less by affidavit
with the written consent of the other heirs (3B:10-4). Both are intestate procedures and both
leave the affiant with an administrator's duties.

**Intestacy.** With no will, the surviving spouse takes the whole estate when every descendant is
also the spouse's and the spouse has no other descendants, or when there is no descendant or
parent; otherwise the spouse takes the first 25% (not less than $50,000 nor more than $200,000)
plus three-quarters of the balance when a parent survives, or plus half the balance in the
blended-family cases (3B:5-3). The rest passes to descendants, then parents, then their
descendants, then grandparents and theirs, then stepchildren (3B:5-4). An heir must survive by 120
hours (3B:5-1).

## The clocks

Compute every one of these from the trigger fact, under the stated rule, against the court's
legal-holiday calendar for the year, and record the rule and its version with the date. A date
with no rule behind it is a guess.

| Clock | Trigger | Rule | Count | Weekend or holiday |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Notice of probate mailed to beneficiaries and next of kin | Probate | R. 4:80-6 | 60 days, probate day excluded (R. 1:3-1) | Runs to the next business day |
| Proof of mailing filed with the Surrogate | The mailing | R. 4:80-6 | 10 days | Runs to the next business day |
| Creditor claim period | Death | N.J.S.A. 3B:22-4 | 9 months | A date the firm waits for; take the later day |
| Will contest (probate or letters) | Probate or letters | R. 4:85-1 | 4 months; 6 months for a person outside the State at the grant; a court may extend 30 days (R. 4:85-2) | A date the firm waits for; take the later reading |
| Inheritance tax return and payment | Death | N.J.S.A. 54:35-3; Division instructions | 8 months to the same day; no corresponding day means the first of the following month | **None.** Interest runs from the eighth-month date even on a weekend |
| Federal estate tax return, Form 706 | Death | 26 U.S.C. § 6075(a) | 9 months; six-month extension on Form 4768 | Allowed by § 7503, but file by the earlier date |

The notice of probate goes to every beneficiary under the will and every person R. 4:80-1(a)(3)
names — the heirs who would take if there were no will — stating that the will was probated, where
and when, the fiduciary's name and address, and that a copy of the will will be furnished on
request. Proof of mailing is filed with the Surrogate within ten days.

Creditors present claims in writing and under oath within nine months of death; a claim presented
later cannot be charged against assets the fiduciary has already properly paid out (3B:22-4). That
period is why nothing is distributed early, and why the firm keeps a register of claims with the
date each was received, compared against the computed end of the period. Whether a claim is
allowed, disputed or rejected is the fiduciary's decision on the attorney's advice, and the
register records it.

## Inheritance tax, waivers and the federal return

**The return.** The transfer inheritance tax is owed by class of beneficiary (Class A exempt;
Class C and D taxed; Class E exempt — `counsel:estate-plan-design` has the rates), and the return
and payment are due eight months after death with interest from that day. The Division grants
extensions to file, never to pay. A resident decedent with only Class A beneficiaries may need no
return but still needs waivers.

**Waivers.** New Jersey real estate, and accounts and securities held in a New Jersey institution,
cannot be transferred without a tax waiver from the Division or a self-executing waiver form.
Class A beneficiaries use the self-executing forms (Form L-8 for accounts and securities, Form L-9
for real estate); anyone else waits for the Division's waiver after the return. The lien runs
fifteen years (54:35-5). Plan the waivers at the start; they are the reason an estate with a
non-Class A beneficiary takes a year.

**Form 706.** Required when the gross estate plus adjusted taxable gifts exceeds the basic
exclusion amount for the year of death ($15,000,000 for 2026). It is also the only way to elect
portability of a deceased spouse's unused exclusion to the survivor, and the election requires a
timely return — nine months, or within the extension — even where no tax is due. Deciding whether
to file a 706 for portability is an attorney's judgment made early, because the deadline is short
and the late-election relief is not guaranteed. Record the decision either way.

## Commissions and the accounting

A fiduciary may take, without court allowance, 6% of income received (3B:18-13) and corpus
commissions of 5% on the first $200,000, 3.5% on the next $800,000 and 2% above $1,000,000, plus 1%
of corpus for each additional fiduciary, no one fiduciary taking more than a sole fiduciary would
(3B:18-14). A beneficiary adversely affected may ask the court to reduce them on a showing that the
services were materially deficient.

Most estates settle on an informal accounting: a statement of what came in, what went out, what
each beneficiary receives, delivered to every beneficiary with a request for a release. A formal
accounting in the Superior Court is for an estate where someone objects or a fiduciary wants a
judgment.

## Distribution and release

On paying a devise or distributive share, the fiduciary takes a refunding bond from the recipient
and files it with the Surrogate (3B:23-24); the bond is the beneficiary's promise to return the
share if a debt later surfaces. Pair it with a release of the fiduciary. No distribution before
the creditor period has run and the tax waivers are in hand, except where the fiduciary has
reserved for every known claim and the attorney has advised in writing that a partial distribution
is safe.

## Never

- Distribute before the creditor claim period has run and the waivers are in hand, without a written reservation analysis.
- Type a deadline into the calendar instead of computing it from the trigger, the rule and the holiday calendar.
- Treat the inheritance tax due date as carrying over a weekend; it does not.
- Let a portability election lapse because no federal tax was due.
- Represent the beneficiaries when the client is the fiduciary.
- Pay a fiduciary commission that exceeds the statute without a court order.
