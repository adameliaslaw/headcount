# Attorney review and release

> Written for a law practice, where a licensed attorney makes every call that is theirs. The
> rules named are New Jersey's; another state's differ, and the attorney admitted there decides.
> Nothing here is advice to a member of the public.

Reviewer-class inside the practice: the attorney who reviews is not the agent or assistant who
drafted, and the drafter does not release. A review that the producing step performs on itself is
not a review.

## The rule

Nothing reaches a client, a court, a bank, a title company, a government office or another party
until the responsible attorney has read the exact version that will go, and the attorney's act of
release is what sends it. No schedule, template, agent, integration or "send on approval" setting
sends on its own. Approval in a conversation is not release; the release is the click or the
signature on the version reviewed.

Tie the review to the exact saved revision. A later edit — any edit, including a date — requires a
new review. The failure this prevents is the common one: the attorney read Tuesday's draft, the
assistant fixed a typo Wednesday and changed a share by accident, and Wednesday's version went out
under Tuesday's approval.

The New Jersey Supreme Court's 2026 model AI policy for firms states the same rule for AI-assisted
content: no AI-assisted work product goes to a client, opposing counsel or the court without lawyer
review and editing, every citation is verified against an official source, and an unverifiable
citation is removed. ABA Formal Opinion 512 (2024) reaches the same place from the duty of
competence. Treat generated text as a draft from a capable, unsupervised junior who has never met
the client.

## Read for the failure, by document type

A review that reads for tone and typos misses what actually goes wrong. Read for the specific
failures each document type produces.

**Will.** Testator's full legal name and domicile. Every beneficiary named as the client named
them, with the relationship stated. The dispositive scheme as the client described it — per
stirpes or per capita, and which one the client actually meant. Specific gifts, and what happens if
the asset is gone. The residuary clause exists and disposes of everything. Fiduciary nominations
with successors: executor, trustee of any testamentary trust, guardian of minors. Tax apportionment
clause, and whether it does what the plan assumes. Execution block matches N.J.S.A. 3B:3-2, with
the self-proving affidavit under 3B:3-4. No clause from a prior plan or a different client survived
the assembly — search for every name that should not be there.

**Revocable trust.** Settlor, initial trustee and successor trustees, with the incapacity mechanism
that moves authority. Beneficiaries during the settlor's life and after death, consistent with the
will's pour-over. Distribution standards stated the way the client wants them. Trust protector or
amendment powers if any, and who holds them. The schedule of assets, if any, matches the funding
plan. For a joint trust, both settlors' elections are recorded and the document renders both
names and both signature blocks — a joint instrument that fell back to a single-settlor variant is a
known failure mode of templated drafting.

**Power of attorney.** Durable language per N.J.S.A. 46:2B-8.2(b), or the plan's incapacity
provisions fail. Gifting authority stated explicitly if intended, and bounded. Authority over
retirement accounts, real estate, digital assets and the trust (to fund it) if the plan needs them.
Agent and successor agents. Execution block provides for acknowledgment before a notary (46:2B-8.9).

**Advance directive for health care.** Health care representative and alternate. Instruction
directive consistent with what the client said. Execution block provides for either two adult
witnesses, neither of whom is the representative, or acknowledgment before a notary, attorney or
other oath officer (N.J.S.A. 26:2H-56).

**Deed.** Grantor exactly as on the vesting deed. Grantee exactly as the trust or buyer is named.
Legal description copied from the prior deed, not retyped. Consideration stated, and the Affidavit
of Consideration claims the exemption the facts support. Preparer, acknowledgment, and the
mailing address for tax notices.

**Estate administration letters and filings.** The right court and county. The right capacity
(executor, administrator, administrator with the will annexed). Dates computed by the engine and
their rule versions stated, never typed. The notice of probate lists every person R. 4:80-6
requires. Amounts in a refunding bond match the distribution.

**Tax returns and schedules.** Every figure traces to a document in the file. Beneficiary class is
right for each transferee. The return is the current form revision.

**Correspondence.** The right recipient and nobody else on the copy line. No fact stated that is
not in the file. No advice given to a non-client. No deadline stated that was not computed.

**AI-assisted anything.** Every citation checked against the official source. Every fact checked
against the file. Every number recomputed. Style is the attorney's own; the model's register is
not the firm's.

## Make the review inspectable

Record who reviewed, which revision, and when, as a receipt attached to the document rather than as
a note in a conversation. A reviewer stamp that binds to the revision's content — a hash, a version
number, an immutable save — is what lets the firm answer later which version was released and on
whose authority.

The one record every act of the practice is written to should carry the review and the release as
separate events, with the actor on each. Two events, because they are two acts.

## Under time pressure

A closing date, a court date or a client in a hospital bed does not change the rule; it changes the
schedule. Read the document. If there is not time to read it, there is not time to send it, and the
attorney says so to whoever is waiting. The deadline pressure that feels like a reason to skip
review is the condition under which review is most likely to catch something.

## Never

- Release a document the attorney has not read in the exact version that will go.
- Let an approval given on one revision carry over to a later edit.
- Allow any schedule, agent or integration to send on the firm's behalf.
- Pass an AI-generated citation without checking it against the official source.
- Skip the review because the deadline is close.
- Let the step that drafted a document be the step that reviews it.
